**08   THE FOOTBALL DATA MODEL** 

**A structured football ontology will define how the platform represents the game.** 

**Game context:** 

Opponent, period, clock, score and possession. 

**Situation:** 

Down, distance, field position and personnel. 

**Pre-snap structure:** 

Formation, alignment, motion, front and shell. 

**Post-snap behavior:** 

Routes, blocking actions, rotation and pressure. 

**Outcome:** 

Gain or loss, completion, sack, turnover and other events. 

**Evidence metadata:** 

Source clip, timestamps, confidence and review status. 

**A canonical terminology layer should support team-specific vocabulary without losing consistency across datasets.**

Evaluate coverage hypotheses through defender movement and receiver relationships. 

Analyze visible run-fit behavior and pursuit. 

Track secondary shells, rotations and changes in leverage. 

Distinguish rushers, simulated pressure, delayed pressure and dropping defenders. 

Compare pre-snap presentation with post-snap structure. 

**Coverage identification must account for disguise, match rules and assignment ambiguity.**
